🕵️‍♂️ Misleading Graphs

The Detective Challenge: Evidence Review

Case 1: The Pet Store Scale

Evidence: The y‑axis starts at 40. School B looks 4x larger than School A, even though the difference is only 8 pets.

(a) Starting at 40 exaggerates small differences. (b) Start the y-axis at 0 so heights match actual differences.

Case 2: The Video Game Timeline

Evidence: Labels for 2019, 2022, and 2024 are evenly spaced, but the time between them is not equal (3 years vs 2 years).

(a) Hides when changes happen and can make trends look faster than they are. (b) Label every year (2019-2024) with even spacing.

Case 3: PE vs. Art Design

(a) Color and 3D effects draw attention to one option, making it feel more popular or important. (b) Use neutral, identical styles for both.

Case 4: The News Scale Battle

(a) Graph B zooms in on the 50-60% range, making a small 4% change look like a huge spike. (b) Graph A is more honest.

Case 5: The "Cherry Picked" Gym

(a) Hides the rest of the year where sales usually drop. (b) Trustworthy graphs show the full context.

Case 6: The Giant Bus

(a) The wider bar has more total area, tricking the eye. (b) All bars must have equal width.

Mission Complete: Detective Summary

You've seen the tricks. Now, summarize your knowledge.

  • Truncated Baselines: Starting Y-axis at a number other than 0.
  • Inconsistent Scales: Uneven spacing on X or Y axes.
  • Cherry Picking: Using only a small part of the data.
  • Visual Distortion: Using 3D effects or inconsistent widths.
  • Missing Labels: Hiding the units or context of the data.
