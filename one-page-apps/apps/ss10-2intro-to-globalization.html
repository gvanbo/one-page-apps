Unit 1: Should Globalization Shape Identity?

Week 1: Introduction to a Globalizing World

What is Globalization?

Have you ever heard the phrase, "It's a small world"? That phrase describes exactly what we are studying!

Globalization means countries are becoming more dependent on each other. The world's citizens are becoming increasingly connected.

How does it happen?

  • Cultures are shared worldwide.
  • Goods are transported across borders.
  • Communication is instant around the globe.

Connecting to Your Identity

In Unit 1, we will explore how these global connections impact you. Throughout this course, always ask yourself: Does this change who I am?

Economic Globalization

The economic area of our lives involves handling money, transferring possessions, and providing services.

Economic Globalization means a country’s economy is influenced heavily by other countries as goods are traded, imported, and exported.

Key Terms to Know:

Consumer

Any person who buys products. (That includes you!)

Transnationals

A company that operates in two or more countries (like Walmart).

Social Globalization

Social globalization looks at how our daily lives and cultures are shared and influenced across the globe.

How does it affect you?

  • The media (communication networks that reach large amounts of people) influences our culture.
  • We watch movies filmed in the United States, eat foods from around the world, and listen to international music.

Connecting to Your Identity

Think about your individual identity (what makes you unique) and your collective identity (the groups you belong to). How many parts of your identity would be different if you had never seen a movie, heard music, or worn clothes created outside of your community?

Political Globalization

Political globalization is evident in the number of international agreements that exist in the world today.

Canada's Global Role:

Canada is part of several international groups that work together to make decisions. These include:

  • UN (United Nations): Representatives from different countries learn about one another and work together as a global society.
  • NAFTA (North American Free Trade Agreement): An agreement between Canada, the USA, and Mexico to reduce trade tariffs so trade can happen quickly.
  • NATO (a military alliance) and the G8.
