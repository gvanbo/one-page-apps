Lesson 6: Cultural Connections

Read or Listen

Indigenous people have a strong connection to the land because they believe the land is like a family member that takes care of them. They give special names to landmarks to remember important stories, animals, and plants that live there. These names help them know and respect the land better

The Rocky Mountains in Alberta are a very special natural landmark. People like to go hiking and skiing in the mountains.

The Indigenous name for the Rockies is 'Kootenay'.

Lake Louise is a stunning blue lake surrounded by mountains.
It is a popular spot for visitors and outdoor activities.

The Indigenous name for Lake Louise is 'Lake of the Little Fishes'.

The Alberta Prairies are large, flat grasslands that cover the southern part of Alberta.
They are known for their wide-open spaces, tall grasses, and abundant wildlife.

In Cree it's called 'kisiskihtin', which means "place where the sky meets the earth".

The Bow River is an important river that flows through Alberta.
It provides water for animals and plants and is great for fishing.

The Indigenous name for the Bow River is "Mihkac"

The Badlands are a unique, dry areas with interesting rock formations.
They are home to many fossils and historical sites.

The Indigenous name for this area is "Mikisiw" (meaning "Eagle").

The Cypress Hills are known for their forests and diverse wildlife.
They are the highest point in Canada between the Rockies and the Atlantic.

The Indigenous name for Cypress Hills is "Ka-ki-oh-sik" which means to make a lot of noise.

Okotoks Rock has significance to the Blackfoot people of Alberta.
It is an enormous glacial boulder that slid from the mountains to Okotoks.

The Indigenous name for the rock is "okotok," which means "rock".
